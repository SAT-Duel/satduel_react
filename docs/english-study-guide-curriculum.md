# SAT English study guide curriculum

## Product goal

Teach the Digital SAT Reading and Writing section as a set of repeatable reading, reasoning, revision, and editing decisions. The guide must help a student building foundations and still give a 700+ scorer sharper ways to handle subtle evidence, scope, precision, and complex syntax.

The curriculum follows the current College Board framework:

- 54 questions in two 32-minute modules, with one discrete question per short passage or passage pair.
- Craft and Structure (about 28%), Information and Ideas (about 26%), Standard English Conventions (about 26%), and Expression of Ideas (about 20%).
- Questions are grouped by domain and generally progress from easier to harder within each skill grouping.
- Passages range from 25 to 150 standardized words and draw from literature, history/social studies, humanities, and science. Topic-specific prior knowledge is not required.

## English lesson learning loop

Every English lesson uses the same cognitive sequence while changing the text and decision being trained.

1. **Diagnose** — a short opening scenario exposes the learner's current instinct before instruction.
2. **Learn the lens** — two or three concise concepts explain what the question is really measuring and the decision rule that transfers.
3. **See the reasoning** — one fully worked SAT-style example begins with a prediction, then audits all four answer choices.
4. **Keep the rule** — a compact transfer rule and a 700+ refinement separate the durable idea from the example.
5. **Practice for transfer** — four original questions progress from foundation to core SAT to advanced/700+ complexity. Explanations address every choice, not only the correct one.
6. **Reflect** — the student classifies the miss (task, text, prediction, choice, or timing) and writes one next-time rule.

This is a model–practice–reflect cycle. It alternates worked examples with problem solving, uses active retrieval, and asks deep explanatory questions instead of rewarding answer memorization.

## Curriculum

### Module 1 — The SAT Reading Method

The operating system for every later lesson. This module is complete in the first release.

1. **The Digital SAT English Map** — format, domain order, adaptation, pacing, and what one-question passages change.
2. **Read the Task Before the Text** — translate recurring stems into a precise job before reading.
3. **The Textual Contract** — require direct support, match scope and strength, and avoid outside knowledge.
4. **Predict Before You Pick** — form a plain-language target before answer choices create noise.
5. **Distractor Anatomy** — identify true-but-irrelevant, half-right, reversed, too-strong, and unsupported choices.
6. **Review That Raises Your Score** — diagnose the decision that failed and write a transfer rule that changes future behavior.

### Module 2 — Words, Purpose, and Structure

College Board domain: Craft and Structure.

1. Vocabulary by context and prediction
2. Precision, connotation, and register
3. Sentence function: role, not topic
4. Main purpose and text structure
5. Cross-text agreement and response
6. 700+ craft: qualification, concession, and subtle alignment

### Module 3 — Main Ideas, Inferences, and Evidence

College Board domain: Information and Ideas.

1. Central ideas without over-summarizing
2. Details and accurate paraphrase
3. Inferences: what must be true
4. Literary inference and character
5. Textual evidence: support and challenge
6. Quantitative evidence and informational graphics
7. 700+ evidence: experiments, competing claims, and multiple results

### Module 4 — Sentence Boundaries

College Board domain: Standard English Conventions — Boundaries.

1. Find the sentence core: clauses and complete thoughts
2. Periods and semicolons
3. Commas with coordinating conjunctions
4. Colons and dashes
5. Essential and nonessential elements
6. 700+ boundaries: long subjects, interruptions, and no-punctuation traps

### Module 5 — Form, Structure, and Sense

College Board domain: Standard English Conventions — Form, Structure, and Sense.

1. Subject-verb agreement
2. Verb tense and verb form
3. Pronouns, plurals, and possessives
4. Modifiers and logical subjects
5. Parallelism and comparisons
6. 700+ grammar: subtle agreement in early-college-level sentences

### Module 6 — Transitions and Rhetorical Synthesis

College Board domain: Expression of Ideas.

1. Name the relationship before the transition
2. Contrast, continuation, cause, and example
3. Concession, clarification, emphasis, and specification
4. Translate the rhetorical goal
5. Select only the notes the goal needs
6. 700+ synthesis: audience, generalizations, and specific study results

### Module 7 — Timed Mastery

Integrates every domain under testing conditions.

1. Use the section's predictable order
2. Two-pass triage and pacing
3. Hard Module 2 precision
4. Mixed-domain mini-module
5. Final error audit and 14-day maintenance plan

## Content standards for authored questions

- Use original passages and questions; do not reproduce College Board items.
- Include literature, history/social studies, humanities, and science across a module.
- Make all required information available in the stimulus.
- Keep one single-best-answer key with three plausible distractors tied to named reasoning errors.
- Use four questions per lesson unless a skill genuinely needs fewer; quality and explanation depth matter more than volume.
- Progress difficulty through text complexity, inference distance, competing plausible choices, and syntactic complexity—not obscure trivia.
- Explain why each wrong answer fails and end with a portable rule.
